The problem reduces to finding the maximum height \( h \) such that not all buildings are visible from \((0, h)\). Geometrically, building \( i \) is blocked if the line from \((0, h)\) to its top passes through or below an earlier building. This yields a threshold \( h_i = \max_{j < i} b_{i,j} \), where \( b_{i,j} \) is the y-intercept of the line through the tops of buildings \( j \) and \( i \). Thus the answer is the maximum y-intercept over all pairs of buildings, or \(-1\) if this maximum is negative. We can transform each building to a point \((1/X, H/X)\); then \( b_{i,j} \) becomes the slope between the transformed points. The maximum slope between any two points in a set is achieved by an edge of their convex hull. Therefore, we compute the convex hull of the transformed points and take the maximum slope of its edges.
