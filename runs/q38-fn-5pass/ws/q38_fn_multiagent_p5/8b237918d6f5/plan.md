Use the fact that a shortest path distance of at least D is equivalent to assigning integer potentials d_v in [0,D] with d_1=0, d_N=D and d_v <= d_u+1 for every edge u->v; an edge must be chosen exactly when d_v>d_u. For a fixed D, minimizing the number of chosen edges under these constraints can be modeled as an s-t min cut on a D-layer graph, where node (v,i) means d_v>=i. Infinite-capacity edges enforce monotonicity and the transition constraints, while unit-capacity edges count original edges that cross a layer. Since feasibility is monotone in D and the answer is at most min(K,N-1), binary search or linear scan over D gives the maximum. Exactly K edges is equivalent to at most K because adding extra weight-1 edges cannot decrease the shortest distance.
