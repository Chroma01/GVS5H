Use a combinatorial reduction: among the n-1 adjacent pairs, choose exactly k positions where equality occurs. Collapsing those equal-adjacent runs leaves n-k distinct consecutive blocks; choose the first block’s value in m ways, then each next block must differ from the previous, giving m-1 choices each. Thus the count is C(n-1, k) * m * (m-1)^(n-1-k) modulo 1e9+7. Implement efficient modular combination and exponentiation for n,m up to 1e5. Validate against the examples and edge cases such as k=0, k=n-1, and m=1.
