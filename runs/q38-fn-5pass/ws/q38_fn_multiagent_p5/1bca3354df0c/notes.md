- **Odd N:** A terminal position is a connected complete bipartite graph. If N is odd, every cut size X gives X(N-X) even, so every maximal play has parity M. Therefore Aoki wins iff M is odd.
- **Component data:** For each connected component, let its bipartition sizes be (a,b). Let K be the number of odd-size components, z the number of isolated vertices, and y = K-z the number of non-isolated odd components. Let D be the parity of the total number of missing internal legal edges: D = (sum a*b - M) mod 2.
- **Useful parity invariant:** The parity of sum a*b over all components is the XOR of the parities of even components whose two color classes are both odd. Hence D is also related to M and that XOR. More importantly, define P = (XOR of even-component class parity) XOR D. One can check that every legal move toggles P. Since M also toggles every move and initially P = M mod 2, P is always equal to M mod 2.
- **Case y = 0:** Only isolated vertices are odd components. Let Q = z/2 + D (mod 2). A move of type isolated-isolated or an internal move or an even-even merge toggles Q. A move merging an even component with an isolated vertex creates y=1, which is always winning for the next player. Thus positions with Q=0 are P-positions and Q=1 are N-positions. So Aoki wins iff (z/2 + D) is odd.
- **Case y = 1:** There is one non-isolated odd component and an odd number of isolated vertices. The player to move merges the non-isolated odd component with an isolated vertex. This is a choice merge: it can choose the parity of the resulting internal missing edges, so it can move to a y=0 position with Q=0. Hence y=1 is always winning for the player to move.
- **Case y = 2:** There are two non-isolated odd components. The player to move merges them together. This is also a choice merge and can choose the resulting D parity to make the following y=0 position have Q=0. Hence y=2 is always winning for the player to move.
- **Case y >= 3:** Every move toggles M parity. If M is odd, the player to move has a pass move to M even: if D=1, play an internal edge; otherwise the even-component parity XOR is 1, so there is an even component and merging it with a non-isolated odd component is a legal pass. If M is even, every move makes M odd; critical merges reduce y but cannot jump from y>=3 directly to y=0, they only reach y=1 or y=2 (already winning for the next player) or keep y>=3 with M odd. Pass moves also give M odd. Therefore y>=3 positions are winning exactly when M is odd.
- **Final rule for even N:** If y=0, Aoki iff (z/2 + D) is odd. If y=1 or y=2, Aoki always. If y>=3, Aoki iff M is odd.
- **Implementation:** Color all components iteratively, count a,b per component, accumulate K, z, and sum_ab parity. Compute D = sum_ab_parity XOR (M&1), then apply the rule in O(N+M) time and O(N+M) memory.
- **Superseded material:** Earlier speculative rules based only on K, z, F, or M+C+K/2 were incorrect. The brute-force-inspired counterexample P3+3I is actually winning for Aoki by moving to a y=0 position with Q=0; the corrected case split by y is the key.
