Represent the pieces by their sorted positions in A and the target occupied squares by the sorted positions of 1s in B. A final configuration exists exactly when the gaps between consecutive B-ones can be matched, in order, to gaps between consecutive A-pieces with each matched A-gap at least as large; unmatched A-gaps are shrunk to zero. The minimum operation count is governed by endpoint displacements, total gap reduction, and immutable “exact” matched gaps where the two gaps are equal. If all required B-gaps can be matched to strictly larger A-gaps, the answer is simply the maximum of the endpoint lower bound and half the total gap reduction. Otherwise, all exact matched gaps must share one displacement parity; for each feasible parity, compute the smallest operation count satisfying endpoint bounds, parity, and odd outer-component bounds, then take the minimum.
