Use dynamic programming over subsequence length parity, product, and alternating sum, exploiting the small product limit and small value bound. Since each chosen number is at most 12 and there are at most 150 numbers, the alternating sum is bounded by 12 * 150, so sums can be represented compactly with bitsets. For each product value, store bitsets of reachable alternating sums for even-length and odd-length subsequences, then update by either skipping or appending each number. Track only non-empty subsequences, handle zero specially because it forces product 0 while toggling parity, and finally scan products from limit down to 0 to find the largest valid product.
