First prove that because k >= 4, the answer is never larger than side: any four selected points either put two on one side (distance at most side) or place one on each side, where the four adjacent side distances sum to 4*side. Thus binary search only needs to consider distances d in [0, side]. Map every point to its clockwise perimeter coordinate t in [0, 4*side), and observe that for d <= side, same-side and adjacent-side Manhattan distances equal the shorter perimeter distance, while opposite-side points are automatically at least side apart. Therefore a subset is feasible for d exactly when the clockwise gaps between consecutive selected perimeter coordinates are all at least d. Check this by trying each point as the first chosen point and greedily jumping to the earliest next coordinate at least d ahead in a duplicated coordinate array, accepting if k points fit before start + 4*side - d. Finally binary search the largest feasible d.
