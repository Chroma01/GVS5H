Model pieces in left-to-right order; operations preserve order and never increase gaps between consecutive pieces. A final configuration corresponds to assigning the K pieces from A to the M occupied positions of B, covering every B position at least once, which is equivalent to matching the M-1 gaps of B to an increasing subsequence of the K-1 gaps of A with capacity `d_t >= g_j`. The first and last pieces must land on the first and last `1` of B, giving a fixed endpoint cost `base = max(B_first - A_first, A_last - B_last)`. The only extra cost is a parity penalty of at most 1, caused by gaps that remain unchanged (`d_t = g_j`): all unchanged transition gaps must share one prefix parity, and the parity needed for zero penalty is fixed by the span reduction and endpoint balance. A constant-state DP over the A gaps, tracking whether an unchanged gap has appeared, its parity, and the parity of the matched B-gap sum, decides feasibility and whether the zero-penalty case is achievable.
