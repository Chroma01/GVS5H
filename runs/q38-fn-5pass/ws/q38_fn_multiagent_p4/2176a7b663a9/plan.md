The key observation is that any minimum-weight path in this disjoint-interval graph can be shortened to length at most 3: if the endpoints are disjoint, the direct edge is optimal; otherwise the best path uses either one common neighbor disjoint from both endpoints, or two neighbors, one strictly to the right of one endpoint and one strictly to the left of the other. Thus each query can be answered from the endpoint weights plus the minimum weight of such an intermediate vertex or compatible pair of intermediate vertices. Precompute prefix minima over interval right endpoints and suffix minima over interval left endpoints to query, for any interval, the minimum weight of an interval strictly to its left or strictly to its right. Then process every query in O(1), printing -1 only when all candidate intermediate costs are infinite.
