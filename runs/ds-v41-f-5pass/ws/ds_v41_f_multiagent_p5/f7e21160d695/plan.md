The key is to compute minimax path values efficiently by building a Kruskal reconstruction tree, where f(u,v) equals the weight of the LCA of leaves u and v. This reduces the problem to matching two colored sets of leaves, minimizing the sum of LCA weights. Process the reconstruction tree bottom-up: each subtree keeps only the unmatched surplus of A or B after optimal internal matching. At each internal node, match as many opposite-color surpluses from its children as possible at that node’s weight, then pass the remaining surplus upward. The final accumulated matching cost is the answer.
