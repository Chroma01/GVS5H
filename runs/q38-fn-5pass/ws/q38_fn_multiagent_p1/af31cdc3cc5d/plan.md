Model the process as choosing pairs of original positions that are removed when all positions between them have already been removed; thus pairs form a non-crossing matching, and if N is odd the final unmatched position acts as a barrier that no pair may cross or enclose. For any even-length interval, the maximum score is the sum of its largest half minus the sum of its smallest half: this is an upper bound by larger/smaller elements, and it is achievable by marking the largest half positive and the smallest half negative and repeatedly removing adjacent positive-negative pairs. Therefore for even N the answer is this value for the whole array; for odd N, try every possible final unmatched position of odd 1-based index, and add the optimal values of the even prefix to its left and even suffix to its right. Compute all even-prefix and even-suffix values in O(N log N) by scanning with two heaps that maintain the lower and upper halves and their sums.
