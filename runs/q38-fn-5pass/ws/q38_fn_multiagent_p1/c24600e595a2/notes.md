- **Structural reduction:** Let P be positions with A=1,B=0, Q with A=0,B=1, and R with A=B=1. Positions with A=B=0 are never useful to flip. Each P and Q is flipped exactly once. Each R may be flipped twice as 1->0 then 0->1, or not at all; multiple pairs on the same R are dominated by deleting the inner on/off pair. Thus the only choice is how many and which R positions receive one extra off/on pair.
- **Canonical order:** In an optimal sequence all off flips (P plus chosen R) occur before all on flips (Q plus chosen R). Swapping an adjacent on-flip before an off-flip reduces cost by the on-flip weight. Off flips are sorted by decreasing C; on flips are sorted by increasing C. For a fixed number s of chosen R positions, the optimal choice is the s largest weights in R.
- **Cost formula:** Let S0 be the initial weighted sum of A. For an off multiset X and on multiset Y, with p=|X|, q=|Y|, sumX=sum(X), define F(X)=sum over X sorted descending of x*(p-rank+1), equivalently sum over X sorted ascending of x*rank. Define G(Y)=sum over Y sorted ascending of y*(q-rank+1). The total cost of the canonical sequence is (p+q)*S0 - q*sumX - F + G.
- **Initial values:** Sort P and Q ascending. Initial F is sum P[i]*(i+1). Initial G is sum Q[i]*(q0-i). Initial sumX is sum(P), p=p0, q=q0. This gives the cost for s=0.
- **Efficient enumeration:** Sort R descending and add its weights one by one, representing choosing the largest s R weights. Because previously added R weights are >= the current weight w, strict-less elements in the current off/on multisets come only from P and Q. Maintain pointers idxP and idxQ into sorted P and Q for counts and prefix sums of elements strictly smaller than w, moving them left while P[idx-1] >= w or Q[idx-1] >= w.
- **Updates:** When adding w to X, F increases by sumX - sumP_less + w*(cntP_less+1). Then sumX and p increase. When adding w to Y, G increases by sumQ_less + w*(q - cntQ_less + 1), using q before increment. Then q increases. Recompute total and keep the minimum.
- **Ties:** Strict-less pointers and rank-sum formulas handle equal weights correctly; equal elements contribute consecutive coefficients, and the aggregate is independent of their internal order.
- **Complexity and types:** Sorting dominates with O(N log N); enumeration is O(N). Python integers avoid overflow; the answer can be around 1e17.
- **Edge cases:** If P and Q are empty, the answer is 0 even if R is nonempty, because optional R pairs only add positive cost. Empty P/Q lists and prefix arrays are handled naturally.
