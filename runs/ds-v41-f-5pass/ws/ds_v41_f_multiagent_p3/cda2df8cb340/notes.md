- **Problem:** Compute \(\sum_{i \le j} f(A_i + A_j)\) where \(f(x)\) is the odd part of \(x\) (remove all factors of 2).
- **Reduction to ordered pairs:** Let \(T = \sum_{i,j} f(A_i + A_j)\) over all ordered pairs, and \(D = \sum_i f(2A_i) = \sum_i \text{odd\_part}(A_i)\). Then answer \(= (T + D) / 2\).
- **Key identity:** \(f(x) = x - \sum_{t \ge 1} (x / 2^t) \cdot [2^t \mid x]\). Summing over ordered pairs gives \(T = S_0 - \sum_{t \ge 1} S_t / 2^t\), where \(S_0 = 2N \sum A_i\) and \(S_t = \sum_{2^t \mid (A_i+A_j)} (A_i + A_j)\).
- **Computing \(S_t\):** For \(M = 2^t\), group elements by residue \(r = A_i \bmod M\). For each present residue \(r\), let \(s = (-r) \bmod M\). The ordered pairs with first index in group \(r\) contribute \(\text{cnt}[s] \cdot \text{sum}[r] + \text{cnt}[r] \cdot \text{sum}[s]\). Summing this over all present \(r\) yields exactly \(S_t\) because the first index's residue is unique. Self-pairs (\(r = s\)) are handled automatically.
- **Range of \(t\):** Since \(A_i \le 10^7\), the maximum sum is \(2 \cdot 10^7\), so \(t \le \lfloor \log_2(2 \cdot 10^7) \rfloor = 24\). For larger \(t\), \(S_t = 0\).
- **Complexity:** \(O(N \cdot K)\) time with \(K \le 24\), and \(O(N)\) extra space. Using dictionaries for residues keeps the work proportional to the number of distinct residues (at most \(N\)) per level.
- **Pitfalls avoided:** Use right-shift \(S_t \gg t\) instead of integer division by \(2^t\) (valid since \(S_t\) is divisible by \(2^t\)). The diagonal term uses odd part of \(A_i\), not \(f(A_i)\). Python's negative modulo is safely handled with `(-r) & mask`.
