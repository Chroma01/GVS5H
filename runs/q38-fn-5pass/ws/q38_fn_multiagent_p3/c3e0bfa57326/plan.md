The operation on a balanced substring is mirror-reversal of the corresponding forest of sibling subtrees, so the unordered rooted tree shape (with a super-root for the top-level forest) is invariant. Conversely, two adjacent sibling trees A and B can be swapped while restoring their internal orders: apply the operation to the substring AB, then apply it to each of the two resulting trees separately. Thus arbitrary permutations of children at every node are reachable, and no other tree shape changes are possible. Therefore reachable strings are exactly all ordered parenthesis trees with the same unordered rooted tree shape as the input. Count them as the product over all nodes of the multinomial coefficient for ordering its children, grouping children by unordered-subtree isomorphism class, modulo 998244353.
