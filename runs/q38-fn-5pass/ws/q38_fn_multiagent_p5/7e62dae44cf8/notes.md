- **Model:** Takahashi's absorbed cells always form one connected region. The only cells that can ever be absorbed next are the unabsorbed cells adjacent to this region; absorbing a cell removes it and adds its unabsorbed neighbors to this frontier.
- **Greedy choice:** Keep the frontier in a min-heap by slime strength. If the smallest frontier strength `s` fails `s * X < total`, every other frontier cell has strength at least `s` and also fails, so no legal action exists. If it passes, absorbing it is safe: it increases total strength and only enlarges the frontier, so it cannot invalidate any future legal absorption.
- **Optimality:** All strengths are positive. Any legal sequence can be reordered to absorb the current minimum frontier cell first, because that cell is legal and remains legal after any other absorption. Repeating this exchange argument shows the greedy closure is maximal.
- **Integer comparison:** The condition is strict: `s < total / X`. With positive integer `X`, use `s * X < total`. This avoids floating point. Python handles products up to about `1e21` and totals up to about `2.5e17` easily.
- **Implementation:** Use a flat index `r * W + c`, a strength list, and a `bytearray` state: 0 unseen, 1 already in heap, 2 absorbed. Push each cell at most once when it first becomes frontier. Heap entries are `(strength, index)`. Pop the minimum, skip stale entries, stop on the first non-absorbable live entry, otherwise absorb and push unseen neighbors.
- **Boundary handling:** For flat index `idx`, up/down are checked by `idx >= W` and `idx < N - W`; left/right by `idx % W != 0` and `idx % W != W - 1`. This works for `H = 1` or `W = 1`.
- **Complexity:** Each cell is pushed and popped at most once, so time is `O(HW log(HW))` and memory is `O(HW)`, suitable for `250000` cells.
- **Edge cases:** Single cell outputs initial strength; large `X` may make no absorption possible; strict inequality means equality is not absorbable; stale heap entries are skipped before the stopping condition.
