The problem reduces to choosing a target frequency k and a subset S of letters to keep, where every letter in S ends with exactly k occurrences. For a fixed k and S, the optimal cost can be computed by allowing only changes from a letter to its immediate next letter, since moving a character two or more steps costs at least as much as deleting it and inserting a new character. The cost is the sum of absolute differences between current and target counts, minus savings from shifting excesses into adjacent deficits. A dynamic program over the 26 letters can then select the best S for each k, and we iterate k from 1 to n (plus k=0 for deleting everything). The global minimum over all k is the answer.
