- **Transformation:** Let p_1=0 and p_{v+1}=p_v+w_v. For a person with S<T on interval [l,r], the requirements are p_l=p_r and p_x>p_l for every interior town x. For S>T, they are p_l=p_r and p_x<p_l. Thus each person is an interval [l,r] with a sign/type: U means interior strictly above endpoints, D means interior strictly below endpoints.
- **Forbidden pairs are necessary:** If two intervals share a left endpoint l and have r1<r2, then r1 is an interior point of the longer interval, but the shorter interval forces p_{r1}=p_l, contradicting the strict sign of the longer interval. The same argument holds for shared right endpoints. If two same-type intervals strictly cross, l1<l2<r1<r2, then for U we get p_{l1}=p_{r1}, p_{l2}>p_{l1}, p_{l2}=p_{r2}, p_{r1}>p_{l2}, a cycle; for D the inequalities reverse and also form a cycle.
- **Forbidden pairs are sufficient:** Contract all equalities p_l=p_r. Because no two active intervals share a left or right endpoint, every equality component is a monotone chain of towns v0<v1<...<vk where each consecutive pair is an interval. No component can contain a vertex in the interior of one of its own intervals; otherwise the first edge of the chain from that endpoint would share a left or right endpoint with the interval. Same-type intervals are therefore laminar. Build a directed graph on components: a U interval edge C gives C -> D for every component D meeting its open interval; a D interval edge C gives D -> C. This graph is acyclic. If a directed cycle existed, choose a witnessing interval of minimum length. The cycle must cross the boundary of the set of components meeting that interval's interior. The first crossing edge either crosses the chosen interval with the same type (forbidden), shares an endpoint (forbidden), or yields a shorter witnessing interval, contradiction. A topological order gives integer component potentials, hence integer road strengths. Strict inequalities become positive integer stamina differences.
- **Query reduction:** A range is infeasible iff it contains a forbidden pair. For each person i, compute nxt[i], the smallest j>i forming a forbidden pair with i. Then [L,R] is feasible iff min_{i in [L,R]} nxt[i] > R.
- **Computing nxt:** Sweep j from 1 to M. Maintain active previous people that have not yet received a conflict. By construction, active people contain no forbidden pair. When j arrives, remove every active person conflicting with j and set its nxt to j. Same-endpoint conflicts are found directly with active_left and active_right arrays. Same-type crossing conflicts are found with two segment trees per type: one keyed by right endpoint storing minimum left endpoint, to detect l_i<l_j<r_i<r_j; one keyed by left endpoint storing maximum right endpoint, to detect l_j<l_i<r_j<r_i. Repeatedly find and remove one conflict until none remains. Each person is removed at most once, so total work is near-linear in M log N.
- **Complexity:** Time O((N+M+Q) log N), memory O(N+M).
- **Status:** The forbidden-pair characterization is true, and the implemented sweep plus range-minimum queries solves the problem.
