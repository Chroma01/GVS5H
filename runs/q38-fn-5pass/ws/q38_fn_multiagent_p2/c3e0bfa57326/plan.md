The operation on a valid substring is exactly a mirror of the corresponding forest of sibling subtrees: it reverses their order and mirrors each subtree. Since a single subtree can be mirrored directly, and two adjacent siblings can be swapped while restoring their internal strings by mirroring both back, the generated moves allow arbitrary independent permutations of the children of every node. These moves never change the underlying unordered rooted tree, and every ordering of that unordered tree is reachable. Thus the answer is the number of distinct ordered parenthesis strings obtainable by permuting children arbitrarily, which can be computed bottom-up by multiplying, for each node, the multinomial coefficient for ordering its children among identical unordered child types.
