The minimum possible cost is only 1, 2, 3, or impossible. Cost 1 occurs exactly when some interval is \([1,N]\) and is used as operation 1. Cost 2 occurs exactly when there is a pair of distinct intervals satisfying one of: their union is \([1,N]\) (both operation 1), their intersection is empty (both operation 2), or one contains the other (operation 1 on the container and operation 2 on the contained interval). If none of these holds, then the intervals are pairwise intersecting and inclusion-free; choosing operation 2 on the intervals with minimum and maximum \(L\), and operation 1 on any third interval, covers all positions, so cost 3 is achievable whenever \(M \ge 3\). Hence after failing cost 1 and cost 2 checks, output 3 if \(M \ge 3\), otherwise output -1.
