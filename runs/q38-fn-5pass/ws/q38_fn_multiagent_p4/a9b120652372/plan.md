Characterize an operation sequence of length `m` by a strictly increasing boundary sequence `g_k` with `g_k ≡ k+m (mod 2)` and `|g_k-k| ≤ m`; the final occupancy at square `k` is determined by how many initial `1`s lie in `(g_{k-1}, g_k]`. Thus we need these intervals to contain at least one `A`-piece exactly when `B_k=1`, and none when `B_k=0`. For fixed `m`, this becomes a greedy feasibility problem over the positions of `1`s in `B`: maintain how many `A`-pieces have been crossed, and for each zero-run check that the current gap between consecutive `A`-pieces is long enough to move inside it. Exact feasibility depends on parity, so “possible in at most `m` operations” is `exact(m) or exact(m-1)`. Binary search the minimum `m`, and print `-1` if the predicate fails for a large safe upper bound.
