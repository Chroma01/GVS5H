For a fixed observer height \(h\), a building is visible iff the ray from \((0,h)\) to its top clears all earlier buildings. For two buildings \(j<i\), the critical height where building \(i\) stops being visible due to building \(j\) is the y-intercept of the line through their tops. A geometric reduction shows that the maximum such critical height over all pairs is attained by adjacent buildings in increasing \(x\)-order, because any intermediate building yields an adjacent pair with at least as large an intercept. Thus we only need the maximum non-negative adjacent-pair intercept; if all are negative, all buildings are visible at height 0 and the answer is \(-1\). Compare fractions exactly using integer arithmetic, then print the winning value with high precision.
