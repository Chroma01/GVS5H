Operations A and B swap an adjacent block of X zeros with a block of Y ones, so every moved 1 shifts by exactly X positions and every moved 0 shifts by exactly Y positions. Therefore, for any reachable string, the ordered sequence of positions of 1s modulo X is invariant, and the ordered sequence of positions of 0s modulo Y is invariant. These two ordered residue sequences are also sufficient: a greedy/normal-form argument shows that repeatedly applying one direction of the operation yields a unique canonical string determined only by those sequences. Thus the problem reduces to checking whether S and T have identical one-residue sequences modulo X and identical zero-residue sequences modulo Y. This can be done in O(N) time with two-pointer scans and O(1) extra memory.
