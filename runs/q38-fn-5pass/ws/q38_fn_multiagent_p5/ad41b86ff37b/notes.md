- **Core reduction:** A kept snowflake is determined by its center vertex c. A neighbor v of c can be a branch vertex with y leaves iff it has at least y neighbors other than c, i.e. deg(v)-1 >= y.
- **Fixed center and y:** For fixed c and y, every neighbor with deg(v)-1 >= y can be used simultaneously. In a tree, the non-center neighbor sets of distinct neighbors of c are disjoint, so there are no conflicts among chosen leaves. Kept size is 1 + (y+1) * count.
- **Per-center optimum:** Sort the degrees of neighbors of c in descending order, ignoring degree 1. If the i-th largest degree is d_i, choosing i branches optimally sets y = d_i - 1 and keeps 1 + i * d_i vertices. Maximize this over all centers and all i.
- **Why degree formula works:** A branch vertex of degree d can contribute itself plus d-1 leaves, totaling d kept vertices. When the minimum chosen branch degree is d_i, each of the i branches can contribute at least d_i kept vertices.
- **Correctness sketch:** Any snowflake centered at c with x branches and y leaves has all branch degrees at least y+1. Thus x is at most the number of neighbors with degree at least y+1, and its size is at most 1 + x(y+1). Taking the x largest-degree neighbors and y = d_x - 1 achieves the bound. Tree structure guarantees disjoint leaf candidates.
- **Complexity:** Building adjacency and degrees is O(N). For each vertex, sorting its neighbor degrees costs O(deg(c) log deg(c)); total worst-case O(N log N). Memory is O(N).
- **Implementation details:** Use 0-based indices. For each center, collect neighbor degrees greater than 1, sort descending, and update best kept size. Early exit if best == N. Skip centers with no possible branch.
- **Edge cases:** The optimal center may be an original leaf, as in a star. Degree-1 neighbors cannot be branches because y must be positive. Paths of length 2 or 3 are handled correctly. The problem guarantees at least one valid snowflake, and for N >= 3 best will be at least 3.
