- **Model:** Only letter frequencies matter. For a chosen positive target frequency `f`, each of the 26 letters is either absent with final count `0` or present with final count `f`. The empty final string costs `n` and is never optimal for nonempty input because keeping the most frequent letter at its current count costs `n - max_count <= n - 1`.
- **Baseline cost:** For a letter with original count `c`, making it absent costs `c` deletions. Making it present costs `abs(c - f)` deletions or insertions.
- **Change saving:** Changing one character from letter `i` to `i + 1` costs 1 and replaces one deletion at `i` plus one insertion at `i + 1`, saving exactly 1. Moving two or more letters costs at least 2, equal to or worse than delete plus insert, so only adjacent surplus-to-deficit transfers need explicit accounting.
- **Source and sink roles:** With final value `y_i` in `{0, f}`, surplus is `max(c_i - y_i, 0)` and deficit is `max(y_i - c_i, 0)`. A letter is never both a source and a sink under fixed final states, so positive adjacent savings do not compete. For fixed final states, total saving is `sum_i min(surplus_i, deficit_{i+1})`.
- **DP structure:** For each `f`, process letters left to right with two states: previous letter absent (`dp0`) or present (`dp1`). Transition to absent adds `c_i` and no incoming saving. Transition to present adds `abs(c_i - f)` and subtracts the best saving from the previous state. If previous is absent, its supply is its original count; if previous is present, its supply is `max(previous_count - f, 0)`. Current demand is `max(f - c_i, 0)`.
- **Directionality:** Changes only move upward in the alphabet, so left-to-right processing is exact. Letter `z` has no outgoing change edge.
- **Target bound:** Trying `1 <= f <= n` is sufficient. If `f > n` and the final string has at least two present letters, length-changing operations alone cost at least `2f - n > n`, while the answer is at most `n - 1`. If it has one present letter with original count `c`, cost is at least `f - c > n - c`, which is worse than keeping that same letter at frequency `c` and deleting all others.
- **Testing:** The provided examples produce `1`, `0`, and `2`. Edge checks pass: all `z` gives `0` at `f = n`; all same letters gives `0` at `f = n`; zero-count gaps are handled correctly, e.g. `aaac` needs `2` because `a` to `c` has no adjacent saving; insertion-heavy and adjacent-change cases such as `aaab` and `aaaaab` also match expected optimal costs.
- **Complexity:** There are at most `n` target frequencies and 26 letters, with constant work per transition. Time is `O(26 * n)`, and extra space is `O(1)`.
