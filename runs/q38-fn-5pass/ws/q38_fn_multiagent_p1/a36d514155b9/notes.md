- **Core reduction:** A valid arrangement is just a subset of k cells. For any fixed unordered pair of cells, the number of arrangements containing that pair is C(m*n - 2, k - 2), because the remaining k - 2 pieces are chosen from the remaining m*n - 2 cells. Therefore the final answer is that common count multiplied by the sum of Manhattan distances over all unordered cell pairs.
- **Separability of Manhattan distance:** The sum of |r1 - r2| + |c1 - c2| over all unordered cell pairs splits into a row contribution and a column contribution. For rows, each unordered pair of distinct rows contributes its row distance once for every choice of one column in the first row and one column in the second row, giving a factor of n^2. Similarly, each unordered pair of distinct columns contributes its column distance with a factor of m^2.
- **One-dimensional closed form:** For coordinates 1..L, the sum of distances over all unordered coordinate pairs is sum_{d=1}^{L-1} d * (L - d) = L * (L^2 - 1) / 6. Thus the geometric pair-distance sum is n^2 * m * (m^2 - 1) / 6 + m^2 * n * (n^2 - 1) / 6.
- **Modular arithmetic:** The modulus 10^9 + 7 is prime, so division by 6 is performed using modular inverse pow(6, MOD - 2, MOD). The binomial coefficient is computed with factorials and inverse factorials modulo MOD. Since m*n <= 10^5, precomputing factorials up to m*n - 2 is safe and fast.
- **Implementation details:** The solution precomputes factorials only up to total_cells - 2 because that is the largest n needed for the binomial coefficient. It then computes the row and column geometric sums modulo MOD, combines them with n^2 and m^2, and multiplies by the arrangement count.
- **Edge cases:** The formula handles k = 2, where C(total_cells - 2, 0) = 1, and k = total_cells, where C(total_cells - 2, total_cells - 2) = 1. It also handles one-dimensional grids because the zero-length dimension contributes 0 through the factor L * (L^2 - 1) / 6. The minimum case total_cells = 2 is handled by factorial arrays of size 1.
- **Complexity:** Time complexity is O(m*n) due to factorial precomputation, and space complexity is O(m*n). With m*n <= 10^5, this is well within limits.
- **Pitfalls:** The pair count must be unordered, matching the examples. Pieces are identical, so arrangements are subsets, not labeled placements. Modular division by 6 must use the modular inverse, not integer division after modulo. Intermediate products should be reduced modulo MOD to keep values bounded.
