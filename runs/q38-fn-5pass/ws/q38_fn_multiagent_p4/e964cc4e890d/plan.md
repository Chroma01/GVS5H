Prove that, because the base edges only go forward, the final graph is strongly connected iff every prefix has at least one added edge entering it from outside, i.e. some black vertex in the prefix is paired with a white vertex to its right. Ordering the black vertices, this becomes a prefix-maximum condition on the permutation assigning white indices to black indices: if `q_i` is the number of whites before black `i+1`, then the maximum assigned white index among the first `i` blacks must exceed `q_i` for all relevant `i`. Reduce the counting to a renewal recurrence over the first occurrence of each distinct `q` value: for a segment `(t, L)`, let `R` be the number of matchings whose first bad prefix is exactly there; then `R = falling(t, L) - sum_{u<t} R_u * falling(t - L_u, L - L_u)`, and the answer is `N! - sum R * (N-L)!`. Compute this triangular recurrence efficiently with an NTT-based divide-and-conquer online convolution, using factorials to rewrite `falling` as `fact[t-L_u] / fact[t-L]`. Handle impossible first/last characters and small edge cases separately, all modulo `998244353`.
