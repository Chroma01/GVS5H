Represent each row by the number of black cells in that row; the row and column conditions are equivalent to these row counts forming a nonincreasing sequence from top to bottom. A given black cell at (x, y) forces row x to have at least y black cells, while a white cell at (x, y) forces row x to have at most y-1 black cells. These constraints are feasible exactly when no white cell lies weakly above-left of a black cell, meaning there is no pair with white (x_w, y_w) and black (x_b, y_b) such that x_w <= x_b and y_w <= y_b. To check this efficiently, process the given cells in increasing row order while maintaining the minimum column among all white cells seen so far, including the current row. If any black cell has column at least that minimum, the answer is No; otherwise it is Yes.
