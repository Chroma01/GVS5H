Convert each string to the sorted positions of its `1` pieces, and view the operation as contracting the ordered gaps between pieces; gaps never increase, and pieces may only merge into stacks. The transformation is therefore an alignment of the gap sequence of `B` into a subsequence of the gap sequence of `A`, where an unmatched `A` gap becomes a stack and a matched gap must satisfy `A_gap >= B_gap`. Let `L = first(B)-first(A)` and `R = last(A)-last(B)`; these give the mandatory endpoint movements, and the remaining cost is determined by how exact matches (`A_gap = B_gap`) split the gap-reduction process into parity components. A greedy left-to-right matcher decides whether an alignment exists with no exact matches, or with all exact matches having prefix parity `0` or `1`; each feasible mode yields a candidate answer, and the minimum feasible candidate is the result. If no mode is feasible, output `-1`.
