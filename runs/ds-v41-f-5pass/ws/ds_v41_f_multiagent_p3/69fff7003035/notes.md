- **Final reduction:** Sum over all permutations is linear over numbers. For a fixed number x of digit length d, its contribution is x times a coefficient C_d that depends only on d. Thus answer = sum_d S_d * C_d, where S_d is the sum of all numbers in [1,N] with d digits.
- **Weight details:** Placing x, the digits after x multiply x by 10^{sum of lengths of the numbers after x}. Only the multiset of digit lengths after x matters. For a subset of size k after x, the number of permutations with exactly that subset after x is k!(N-1-k)!.
- **Generating function:** Let c_l be the count of numbers with l digits and w_l = 10^l mod M. Define W(t) = product_l (1 + w_l t)^{c_l}. For x of length d, remove one factor: P_d(t) = W(t)/(1 + w_d t). Then C_d = sum_{k=0}^{N-1} [t^k]P_d(t) * k! * (N-1-k)!.
- **Linear recurrence for W:** Let D(t) = product_l (1 + w_l t) over all six lengths (degree 6), and R(t) = sum_l c_l w_l * product_{m != l} (1 + w_m t) (degree 5). Then D W' = R W. Comparing coefficient of t^n gives p_{n+1} = inv(n+1) * ( sum_i r_i p_{n-i} - sum_{i>=1} d_i (n-i+1) p_{n-i+1} ), with p_0 = 1.
- **Why all six factors are kept:** Even if c_l = 0, including (1 + w_l t) in D is harmless because the corresponding term in R is zero; the identity R/D still equals W'/W. This keeps D and R small and fixed-size.
- **Division step:** For each active length d, Q(t) = P_d(t) satisfies q_k = p_k - w_d q_{k-1}, q_{-1} = 0. Compute q_0..q_{N-1} and accumulate C_d = sum q_k * k! * (N-1-k)!.
- **Complexity:** O(N) time and O(N) memory. D has degree 6 and R degree 5, so the recurrence has only up to 12 terms per coefficient. No NTT needed.
- **Implementation notes:** Precompute factorials and modular inverses up to N in O(N). Counts c_l and sums S_l are obtained from digit-length ranges. Be careful with indices in the recurrence: p_j = 0 for j < 0, and q only needs p[0..N-1].
- **Verification:** N=1 gives 1; N=2 gives 33; sample N=3 gives 1332. The recurrence also reproduces small brute-force concatenation sums.
