- **Structural reduction:** In any alkane, the vertices of degree 4 form a connected subtree S. Any vertex on a path between two degree-4 vertices has degree at least 2 in the alkane, so it must also be degree 4. Each vertex in S must have original degree at least 4 and at most 4 neighbors inside S. Conversely, any connected vertex set S in the original tree where every vertex has original degree at least 4 and internal degree at most 4 can be extended to an alkane by attaching 4 - deg_S(v) unused neighbors as leaves. The tree property makes these leaves distinct. The resulting alkane has size |S| + sum(4 - deg_S(v)) = 3|S| + 2, so maximizing alkane size is equivalent to maximizing |S|.
- **Reduced problem:** Build the forest H induced by original vertices with degree at least 4. The task becomes finding the largest connected vertex subset of H with maximum degree at most 4. If H is empty, no alkane exists. Otherwise, a single eligible vertex already gives an alkane of size 5.
- **DP on rooted H components:** Root each component of H. Let f[v] be the maximum size of a valid selected connected subset inside the rooted subtree of v that contains v, assuming v's parent is selected. Then v may select at most 3 child branches, so f[v] = 1 + sum of the largest up to 3 f[child]. Let g[v] be the analogous value when v's parent is not selected; then v may select at most 4 child branches, so g[v] = 1 + sum of the largest up to 4 f[child].
- **Why max g is sufficient:** For any optimal connected subset S, take the vertex r in S closest to the component root. Then S is contained in the subtree of r, r's parent is not selected, and every selected child branch is no larger than the corresponding f[child]. Hence |S| <= g[r]. Every g[v] itself constructs a valid subset, so the maximum g over all vertices equals the optimum. Rerooting is not needed.
- **Implementation details:** Use iterative DFS to avoid recursion depth issues. The parent array marks visited vertices in H, with root parent set to -1. Process vertices in reverse DFS order. For each vertex, scan its adjacency list and keep the top four dp values among children, identified by parent[to] == v. This gives O(N) time and memory. The final answer is 3 * max_g + 2, or -1 if max_g remains 0.
- **Edge cases:** If no original vertex has degree at least 4, output -1. A single eligible vertex outputs 5. Vertices with H-degree greater than 4 are handled by selecting only the best 3 or 4 child branches depending on whether the parent is selected. Leaves of the final alkane may be original vertices of any degree, including eligible vertices not in S; acyclicity prevents one outside vertex from being needed as a leaf for two different vertices of S.
