- **Functional model:** Operation with pivot i maps piece position j to j-1 (j>i), j (j=i), j+1 (j<i); the map is non-decreasing and 1-Lipschitz. Final positions = F(p_j) for a composition F of T such maps. Pieces preserve order; pieces merge only when a pivot coincides, and merged groups never split afterward.
- **Gap view:** Internal gaps a_v = p_{v+1}-p_v never increase under any operation. Target occupied squares r_1<...<r_k require partitioning pieces into k consecutive groups mapped to the r_t. The gap separating group t and group t+1 must satisfy a_{S_t} >= req_t = r_{t+1}-r_t, where S_t is the index between the groups.
- **Minimum ops lower bound:** T_min = max(0, r_1-p_1, p_m-r_k). The gap feasibility condition guarantees every middle piece's displacement is covered by T_min, so T_min is achievable up to a parity adjustment.
- **Parity:** A tight boundary (a_{S_t} == req_t) forces T ≡ (p_{S_t} - r_t) (mod 2). All tight boundaries in one solution must share a single parity q. Non-tight boundaries impose nothing. For each q∈{0,1} greedily scan j=1..k-1, picking the smallest v>prev with a_v>=req_j and (a_v>req_j or (p_{v-1}-r_j)%2==q); smallest v is optimal because it leaves the most room. Feasible q's are collected into a set.
- **Answer:** -1 if m<k or no feasible q. Otherwise T_min if its parity is feasible, else T_min+1 (one wasted operation flips the parity).
- **Special case k==1:** no interior boundary; answer = max(0, r-p_1, p_m-r). m<k is impossible.
- **Complexity:** O(N+m+k) per test, O(sum N) overall; two greedy scans (q=0 and q=1). Uses bytes compared to 49 for speed.
