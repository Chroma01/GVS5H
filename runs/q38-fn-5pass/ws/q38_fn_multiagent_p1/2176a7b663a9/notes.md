- **Structural bound:** The graph is the complement of an interval graph. If it contained an induced path on five vertices, the interval graph would contain the complement of that path, which has an induced four-cycle, contradicting chordality of interval graphs. Hence the graph is P5-free. Since all vertex weights are positive, a minimum-weight path can be taken simple and induced, so it has at most four vertices, i.e. at most three edges.
- **Disjoint endpoints:** If `R_s < L_t` or `R_t < L_s`, the endpoints are adjacent. Any longer path adds at least one positive intermediate weight, so the direct path `W_s + W_t` is optimal.
- **Overlapping endpoints, two-edge paths:** A common neighbor of two overlapping intervals must lie completely left of both (`R < min(L_s, L_t)`) or completely right of both (`L > max(R_s, R_t)`). These are queried by `pref[min(L_s, L_t)-1]` and `suff[max(R_s, R_t)+1]`.
- **Overlapping endpoints, three-edge paths:** In an induced four-vertex path between overlapping endpoints, the two internal vertices must be on opposite sides: one left of `s` and one right of `t`, or one right of `s` and one left of `t`. Same-side choices make one internal vertex adjacent to both endpoints, creating a chord and a cheaper two-edge path.
- **Independence of opposite-side minima:** If `s` and `t` overlap, then `L_s <= R_t` and `L_t <= R_s`. Therefore any interval left of `s` is automatically disjoint from any interval right of `t`, and similarly for the other orientation. Thus the best three-edge path in each orientation is the sum of independent minima from the two side sets.
- **Precomputation:** Build `pref[x]` as the minimum weight among intervals with right endpoint at most `x`, and `suff[x]` as the minimum weight among intervals with left endpoint at least `x`. Coordinates are only up to `2N`, so arrays of size about `2N+4` are enough. Strict disjointness uses `pref[L-1]` and `suff[R+1]`.
- **Query formula:** For overlapping `s,t`, compute the minimum intermediate cost among `leftBoth`, `rightBoth`, `leftS + rightT`, and `rightS + leftT`. If it is finite, answer `W_s + W_t + best`; otherwise answer `-1`.
- **Endpoint exclusion:** When endpoints overlap, neither endpoint can appear in the relevant left/right side sets because its own endpoint coordinates violate the strict inequalities. For disjoint endpoints, the direct edge is used and intermediate sets are irrelevant.
- **Edge cases:** Intersections are inclusive, so touching intervals are not adjacent; all inequalities are strict. Empty side sets contribute `INF`. Sums fit easily in Python integers; `INF = 10**18` is far above any valid path sum.
- **Complexity:** Time is `O(N + Q)` after linear prefix/suffix construction. Memory is `O(N)` for interval arrays and coordinate minima. The previous segment-tree, Dijkstra, and batching ideas are unnecessary for this final solution.
