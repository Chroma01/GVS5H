Reduce the pairing problem to selecting exactly \(2K\) cakes and assigning each selected cake one of the three attributes, with the constraint that the number of cakes assigned to each attribute is even. The total price is then the sum of the assigned attribute values, and it is upper-bounded by the sum of the top \(2K\) values of \(M_i=\max(X_i,Y_i,Z_i)\). Take those top \(2K\) cakes as a base solution, assign each an arbitrary best attribute, and check the parity of the three attribute counts. If the parity is already even, the base sum is optimal; otherwise, an optimal correction differs from the base by at most two local modifications: recoloring a selected cake or replacing a selected cake by an unselected cake with a chosen color. Compute the minimum loss for one modification of the needed parity toggle, and for the pair of complementary toggles, using top-two statistics to enforce disjoint cakes, then subtract that loss from the base sum.
