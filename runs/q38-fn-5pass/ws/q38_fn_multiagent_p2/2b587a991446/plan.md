Model a valid swap as the shortest nontrivial closed walk whose support contains both S and T: either a cycle with tree-like attachments to S/T, or a tree with a branching vertex. In such a support, cycle edges can be traversed once while bridge/attachment edges must be traversed twice, so the answer is the minimum Chinese-postman length of a non-path support containing S and T. Compute distances from S and T, decompose the graph into bridges/biconnected components, and evaluate two candidate families: branch candidates at articulation/branching vertices and cycle candidates inside biconnected components. Take the minimum candidate; if no candidate exists, output -1.
