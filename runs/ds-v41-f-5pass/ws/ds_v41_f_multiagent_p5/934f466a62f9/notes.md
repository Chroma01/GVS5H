- **Reduction to coloring:** The max over pairings equals the max over choosing 2K items and assigning each to one of three colors (X/Y/Z) with every color count even, of the sum of assigned coordinates. Forward: assign each pair to its argmax color (even counts, exact value). Backward: pair items inside each color class; every pair's price is the max, hence >= the assigned sums.
- **Baseline:** Sort by M_i = max(X_i,Y_i,Z_i), take top 2K (set T), color each by argmax. Its sum is the largest possible sum of M, call it sumM. The answer is sumM minus a minimal correction.
- **Parities:** With baseline colors, counts are even unless exactly two colors are odd (sum 2K is even). All operations are either a recolor (change one selected item's color) or a swap (drop one selected item, add one outside item); each toggles the parities of exactly two colors, i.e. an edge of the triangle XYZ.
- **Only one or two operations needed:** A solution's parity toggle multiset must XOR to the odd-pair vector {p,q}. In GF(2)^2 the odd-multiplicity edges are either the single edge {p,q} or the pair {p,r}+{q,r} (extra equal edges only add nonnegative loss). Each item is used by at most one operation, so the extracted edge(s) use disjoint items. Applying them to T yields a feasible even coloring whose loss is at most the optimum's loss, and every such application is feasible, so the min over 1-op / 2-op configurations equals the optimal loss. This holds even with M-ties because equal-coordinate recolors cost zero.
- **Costs per color pair {a,b}:** recolor a->b: min over T items of color a of (M_i - val_b(i)); recolor b->a symmetric; swap a-out b-in: (min M over T color a) - (max val_b over outside); symmetric. All costs are nonnegative because every T item's M dominates every outside item's coordinates.
- **Enumeration:** Only conflicts are on shared T items of the third color and shared outside items, so keeping the top C=5 candidates per subtype (by the relevant key) and brute-forcing disjoint pairs between the {p,r} and {q,r} lists is sufficient; C=2 would already provably suffice.
- **Complexity:** O(N log N) per test from sorting; candidate generation uses heap-based nsmallest/nlargest, total bounded by the sum of N <= 1e5.
- **Edge cases:** K=1 falls out correctly (even coloring forces both items the same color, covering all three colors via one recolor). When 2K=N there are no outside items, so swaps vanish. Duplicate cakes are distinguished by index in the disjointness checks.
