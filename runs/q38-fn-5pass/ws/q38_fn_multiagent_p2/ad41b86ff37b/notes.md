- **Core reduction:** In any valid snowflake subgraph, once its center vertex `c` is fixed, every middle vertex must be an original neighbor of `c`. A neighbor `u` can serve as a middle vertex with `y` leaves iff `deg(u) - 1 >= y`, because all neighbors of `u` except `c` may be kept as leaves and all other vertices can be deleted.
- **Fixed center optimization:** For a center `v`, define capacities of its neighbors as `deg(u) - 1`, ignoring zero capacities. If these capacities are sorted descending as `c_1 >= c_2 >= ... >= c_m`, then using exactly `k` middle vertices is feasible with maximum possible `y = c_k`, keeping `1 + k * (c_k + 1)` vertices. The best for this center is the maximum over all positive `k`.
- **Global optimum:** The answer is `N - max_kept`, where `max_kept` is the maximum value over all possible centers and all `k`. Every vertex must be considered as a possible center because the snowflake center is not necessarily a high-degree vertex in the original tree. For example, a star is already a snowflake if one of its leaves is chosen as the center.
- **Tree structure guarantees independence:** Leaf sets chosen for different middle vertices cannot overlap or create conflicts. If two middle vertices adjacent to the same center shared a leaf, or if a middle vertex were adjacent to another middle vertex, the original graph would contain a cycle, impossible in a tree.
- **Complexity:** The sum of all degrees is `2N - 2`. Sorting capacities per center costs `sum deg(v) log deg(v)`, which is `O(N log N)` in the worst case and easily fits for `N = 300000`. Memory is `O(N)`.
- **Implementation details:** The program builds a 1-indexed adjacency list and degree array. It handles degree 1 and degree 2 centers directly to avoid many tiny sorts. For larger degrees, it builds the positive capacity list, sorts it descending, and evaluates all `k`. Small special cases for capacity list lengths 1 and 2 also avoid unnecessary sorting.
- **Edge cases:** Both `x` and `y` must be positive, so neighbors with capacity zero cannot be used as middle vertices. A leaf center whose neighbor has degree 2 gives the minimum snowflake of three vertices. Star trees are handled correctly by considering leaf centers, not only the original high-degree center.
- **Output:** The program prints the minimum number of deletions as `N - best`.
