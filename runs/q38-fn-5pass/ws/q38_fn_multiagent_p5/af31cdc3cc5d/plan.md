Use the fact that in any fully removable even-length segment, the optimal score is the sum of its largest half minus the sum of its smallest half: this is an upper bound because each pair contributes one larger and one smaller element, and it is achievable by pairing high-valued and low-valued positions with a non-crossing adjacent-removal matching. For odd N, the final remaining element cannot be crossed by any pair, so it must be at an odd 1-based index and splits the array into two independent even-length segments. Therefore the answer is the maximum over odd split positions of the even-segment value of the prefix plus that of the suffix. Compute the even-segment value for every even prefix and suffix by maintaining two heaps that store the lower and upper halves of the seen values. Then combine these arrays in linear time.
