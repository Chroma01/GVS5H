Visibility from (0,y) to building i is best when aiming at its top; building i is visible iff the line from (0,y) to (X_i,H_i) is strictly above every earlier top. Solving this inequality gives a threshold for i equal to the maximum y-intercept of the line through tops j and i, i.e. max_{j<i} (H_j X_i - H_i X_j)/(X_i-X_j). The answer is the maximum of these thresholds over all i, or -1 if all thresholds are negative. For each i, maximizing the intercept is equivalent to minimizing the slope from a previous point to (X_i,H_i), and the minimizing previous point lies on the upper convex hull of previous points. Maintain that upper hull with decreasing edge slopes, query the tangent vertex by binary search using exact integer cross products, update the best rational intercept, then insert the current point. This yields O(N log N) time and avoids precision problems in comparisons.
