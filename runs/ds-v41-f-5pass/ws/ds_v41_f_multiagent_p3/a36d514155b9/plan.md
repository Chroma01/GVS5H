Use linearity of expectation over unordered pairs of grid cells: every valid arrangement is a k-subset of the m*n cells, so any fixed pair of cells appears together in C(m*n-2, k-2) arrangements. Thus the total answer is that binomial coefficient multiplied by the sum of Manhattan distances over all unordered cell pairs. Split Manhattan distance into independent row and column contributions: sum over cell pairs of |r1-r2| equals n^2 times the sum over row pairs, and similarly for columns. Compute the row-pair distance sum using the closed form m(m^2-1)/6 and the column-pair distance sum using n(n^2-1)/6. Finally multiply the combined distance sum by the binomial coefficient modulo 1e9+7.
