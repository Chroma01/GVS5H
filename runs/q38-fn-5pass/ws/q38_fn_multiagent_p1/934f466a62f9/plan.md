Convert each pair to the attribute that attains its maximum: the problem is equivalent to selecting 2K cakes and coloring each selected cake by one of three attributes, with every color used an even number of times, maximizing the sum of the chosen attribute values. Ignoring parity, the optimum is to take the 2K cakes with largest max(X,Y,Z) and color each by a maximum attribute. If the color parities are not all even, the defect is two odd colors; from this unconstrained optimum, an optimal parity-feasible solution differs by at most two exchanges (recolor a selected cake or swap it with an unselected cake), because any longer exchange cycle has nonnegative loss. Generate a constant-size candidate set of exchanges from the few cheapest selected removals and highest unselected additions per color, then brute-force all single or compatible pairs of exchanges whose color-parity change fixes the defect. The answer is the unconstrained total minus the minimum loss.
