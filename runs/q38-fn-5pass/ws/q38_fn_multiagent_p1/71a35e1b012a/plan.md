The minimum possible cost is at most 3. Cost 1 occurs exactly when some operation interval is `[1, N]` and we choose type 1. Cost 2 occurs iff two distinct intervals have one of three relations: their union covers `[1, N]`, they are disjoint, or one contains the other; the corresponding types are `(1,1)`, `(2,2)`, or `(1 on container, 2 on contained)`. If neither cost 1 nor cost 2 exists and `M >= 3`, then no pair is disjoint, so all intervals share a common intersection; choosing type 2 on an interval with maximum `L` and an interval with minimum `R` covers everything outside that common intersection, and choosing type 1 on any third distinct interval covers the intersection. Otherwise, if `M < 3` and no cost 1/2 exists, the goal is impossible.
