- **Problem reduction:** Choosing K elements is equivalent to omitting N-K elements; chosen XOR = total_xor ^ omitted_xor. Since C(N,N-K)=C(N,K) <= 1e6, the smaller of K and N-K has at most 1e6 subsets.
- **Algorithm:** Let m = min(K, N-K). Enumerate all m-subsets of A via itertools.combinations. If m==K, maximize XOR of the chosen subset directly. If m==N-K, precompute total XOR of A and maximize total_xor ^ XOR(omitted subset).
- **Edge cases:** K=N gives m=0; combinations(A,0) yields one empty tuple, so the answer is total_xor. K=1 enumerates singletons. Duplicate values in A are distinct indices; itertools.combinations over the value list with duplicates still enumerates all positional combinations, which is correct.
- **Complexity:** Time O(C(N,m) * m), with m = min(K,N-K). The maximum over allowed inputs is about 7.4e6 element operations (e.g., N=23, m=9, C=817190), well within limits. Space O(N) for storing A.
- **Implementation notes:** Use sys.stdin.buffer.read().split() for fast input. Use functools.reduce(operator.xor, comb, 0) to compute subset XOR. Initialize best=0 because all XOR values are non-negative.
- **Correctness:** Every valid selection maps to exactly one enumerated subset, either directly or via its complement, and the complement XOR formula preserves the objective. Therefore taking the maximum over enumerated candidates gives the exact answer.
