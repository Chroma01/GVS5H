Process each possible non-zero last digit `d` from 1 to 9 independently. For a fixed `d`, maintain the counts of remainders modulo `d` for all substrings ending at the previous position. When reading the next digit `c`, extend all previous substrings by updating their remainders with `(r * 10 + c) % d`, add the new one-digit substring, and if `c == d`, add the count of substrings with remainder 0 to the answer. This naturally handles leading zeros and skips substrings ending in `0`. The total complexity is about `9 * n * 9`, which is efficient for `n <= 10^5`.
