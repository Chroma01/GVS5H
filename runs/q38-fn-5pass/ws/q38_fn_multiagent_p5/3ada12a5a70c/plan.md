Because k is at least 4, any selection of k boundary points has two points whose shorter boundary arc is at most side, and Manhattan distance is no larger than that arc, so the answer is at most side. For any candidate distance d <= side, two boundary points have Manhattan distance < d exactly when their mapped perimeter coordinates are within circular distance < d; opposite sides cannot be closer than side. Therefore the problem becomes choosing k points on a circle of circumference 4*side to maximize minimum circular spacing. Binary search the answer and use a greedy circular spacing feasibility test.
