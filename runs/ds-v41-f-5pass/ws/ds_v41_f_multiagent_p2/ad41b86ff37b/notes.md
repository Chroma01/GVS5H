- **Reduction:** A snowflake is fixed by center c and y>=1. Let D=y+1>=2. A neighbor v of c can be an intermediate iff deg(v) >= D. If so, v contributes exactly D kept vertices. Different usable neighbors of c have disjoint non-c neighbor sets because T is a tree, so all such neighbors can be used simultaneously. Thus for fixed c,D the maximum kept size is 1 + D * cnt(c,D), where cnt(c,D) is the number of neighbors v of c with deg(v) >= D.
- **Optimization:** For fixed c, D*cnt(c,D) is maximized at D equal to the degree of some neighbor of c with degree >=2. So process vertices as potential intermediates in descending degree order. Maintain cnt[c] = number of processed neighbors of c. When processing v with degree d, for each neighbor c of v do cnt[c] += 1 and update best = max(best, 1 + d * cnt[c]).
- **Tie handling:** If several neighbors of c have degree d, updating after each edge is safe: after the last such edge, cnt[c] equals the full count of c's neighbors with degree >= d.
- **Leaf-center note:** The center may have degree 1. For example, a star K1,t is a snowflake with center at one of its leaves, intermediate at the star center, and y=t-1 leaves. The sweep naturally considers every neighbor c of a high-degree vertex v as a possible center.
- **Algorithm:** Bucket vertices by degree. For d from maxDeg down to 2, for each v in bucket[d], for each c in adj[v], increment cnt[c] and update best. Answer N - best.
- **Complexity:** Total adjacency scans over vertices with degree >=2 is at most 2(N-1), so O(N) time and O(N) memory. Iterative, no recursion.
- **Edge cases:** D>=2 excludes leaves as intermediates. Extra neighbors and leaves are simply deleted. N>=3 guarantees a valid 3-vertex path, so best>=3. The code still handles an empty sweep safely.
- **Sample verification:** Sample1: best=7, answer=1 (center 4, D=3, two usable neighbors). Sample2: best=3, answer=0. Sample3: best=7 at center 2 with D=2 and three usable neighbors, answer=3.
