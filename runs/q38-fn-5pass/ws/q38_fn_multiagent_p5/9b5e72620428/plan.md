Reduce the problem to choosing a target sum S. Let KA and KB be the numbers of known (non -1) elements in A and B; if KA + KB <= N, the unknown slots can absorb all known values, so the answer is Yes. Otherwise, at least K = KA + KB - N known elements from A and known elements from B must be paired directly with the same sum S, and S must be at least the maximum known value. For a fixed S, the maximum number of such known-known pairs is the sum over values x of min(countA[x], countB[S - x]). Therefore, it is enough to find some pair sum S >= maxKnown whose weighted complementary-pair count is at least K.
