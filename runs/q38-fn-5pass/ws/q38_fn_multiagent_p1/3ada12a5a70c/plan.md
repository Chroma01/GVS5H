The key observation is that for k >= 4 the optimal value is at most side, and for any threshold d <= side two boundary points have Manhattan distance less than d exactly when their shorter perimeter-arc distance is less than d. Thus map every point to its clockwise perimeter coordinate in [0, 4*side) and solve the 1D circular packing problem: choose k coordinates maximizing the minimum circular gap. Binary search the answer; for a candidate d, greedily test whether k points can be placed on the circle with all consecutive gaps at least d. For each possible first point, repeatedly jump to the first point at least d ahead in the doubled sorted coordinate array, and accept if k points fit within one circumference with the final wrap gap also at least d. Precompute jump pointers with two pointers so each check is O(n*k), giving O(n*k*log(side)) total time.
