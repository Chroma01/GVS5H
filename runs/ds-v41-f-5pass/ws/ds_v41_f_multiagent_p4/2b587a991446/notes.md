
## ideation
The core difficulty is that the natural state space is the ordered pair of positions (A,B), with A!=B, giving up to N(N-1) states. A direct BFS is therefore impossible for N up to 2e5. The problem is really about the topology of the graph: two pieces can only exchange their order if there is some cycle that lets them pass each other. Bridges and articulation points act as corridors where the relative order of the pieces cannot be changed. Thus the graph’s biconnected components / block-cut tree are central. A solution must also minimize moves, not just decide feasibility, so it must combine shortest distances from S and T to possible “passing” locations with the cost of exchanging the pieces on a cycle. A major pitfall is that simply finding any cycle is not enough: the cycle must be reachable by both pieces without forcing them to collide, and the paths used to reach the cycle may overlap with the paths used to leave it. The given plan is a plausible direction, but details such as “shortest cycle containing x and y,” articulation-vertex handling, and scheduling the two walks without collision need careful verification.
