For D <= S, the Manhattan distance between any two points not on opposite sides equals their shorter perimeter arc, and opposite sides are automatically >= S >= D; thus feasibility reduces to selecting k points on a circle of circumference 4S with pairwise circular distance >= D, solvable by a greedy two-pointer check. For D > S, at most one point can be chosen per side, so k > 3 is impossible; for k = 3, handle the four combinations of three sides using derived linear constraints and binary searches on sorted side coordinates. Special-case k = 2 by directly computing the maximum Manhattan distance via min/max of x+y and x-y.
