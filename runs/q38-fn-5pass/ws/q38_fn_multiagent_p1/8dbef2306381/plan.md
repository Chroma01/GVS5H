The bad squares are disjoint intervals, so the usable squares are a sequence of good intervals. Because every jump length is at most B, any jump that crosses a bad interval must start within B squares before that interval and land within B squares after it; long good intervals can be handled by a distance test instead of enumerating all squares. Compress the problem to O(MB) important good squares: 1, N, and all good squares within B of 1, N, and each bad-interval boundary. For two important squares in the same good interval, they are mutually reachable by repeated jumps iff their positive distance d satisfies ceil(d/B) <= floor(d/A); otherwise transitions are only direct jumps of length A..B, which may cross bad intervals. Process the important squares in increasing order with a forward DP using these transitions, and answer Yes iff N is reachable.
