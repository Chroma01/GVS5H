
## ideation
The core difficulty is that the number of squares is enormous, up to 10^12, while the forbidden squares are given as only up to 20000 disjoint intervals. A direct graph or BFS over all squares is impossible. The useful structure is that every jump length is at most 20, so interactions with a bad interval are local: to cross a bad interval, a jump must start within B squares before it and land within B squares after it. However, long good intervals still need to be handled without enumerating every square, and the exact jump-length constraints in [A, B] can make some distances unreachable even inside a completely good interval.

A second difficulty is preserving exact reachability when compressing the state space. It is not enough to consider only interval endpoints, because a path may enter or leave a good interval at any good square within B of a bad interval boundary. Also, a jump of length at most B may skip over one or more bad intervals if the total distance is small enough, so transitions must consider actual landing positions, not merely adjacent good intervals. Finally, if A = B or if the allowed jump lengths have a nontrivial common divisor, reachability can have modular restrictions; even when gcd is 1, the lower bound A can create exact-distance constraints.

The given plan of compressing to important good squares near 1, N, and bad-interval boundaries is promising, but it needs careful validation: same-good-interval reachability must be characterized exactly, direct jumps crossing bad intervals must be included, and the compression must not discard necessary entry or exit positions.
