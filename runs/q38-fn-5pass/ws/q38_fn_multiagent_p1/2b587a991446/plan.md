Model the process as a shortest path in the ordered configuration graph of two labeled pieces, but compress the “passing” part to states where the two pieces are adjacent. Between adjacent states, any useful excursion that separates the pieces and makes them adjacent again can be represented by a cost-2 slide along an edge, while moves that keep the pieces adjacent (triangle moves) have cost 1; this gives a weighted graph on ordered edges \((u,v)\) with \(O(M)\) states. Compute exact entry costs from the initial configuration to this adjacent-state graph and exit costs to the target by analyzing the first/last time the pieces become adjacent, using BFS distances from \(S,T\) and shortest paths that avoid the other endpoint to prevent invalid shortcuts. Run Dijkstra on the ordered-edge graph with these entry/exit costs; if no path exists (in particular when the graph is just a path), output \(-1\). Generate transitions near-linearly by optimizing triangle/common-neighbor moves and slide moves (e.g., degree ordering and per-vertex top-two minima) to avoid \(O(\sum \deg^2)\) blowup.
