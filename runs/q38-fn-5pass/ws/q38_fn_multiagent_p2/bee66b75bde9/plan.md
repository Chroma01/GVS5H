A valid coloring is a top-left justified shape: if a cell is black, all cells above and to its left must be black, and if a cell is white, all cells below and to its right must be white. Therefore the partial coloring is feasible iff no already-colored white cell lies weakly northwest of an already-colored black cell, i.e. there is no pair with white row <= black row and white column <= black column. This reduces the problem to a 2D dominance check over the M given colored cells. Sort the cells by row, process white cells before black cells in the same row, and maintain the minimum column seen among white cells so far. If any black cell has column at least that minimum, the answer is No; otherwise it is Yes.
